# 3D journal

Press **J** while at rest to bring out a physical book and lower the camera toward its pages. It is available in the hub, the slot room, and between battle actions. Click the edge tabs for combat cards, special cards, event cards, effects, mechanics, profile, and Collection; click an index entry to read it. The left page arrows or mouse wheel browse the index, and longer entries have their own page arrows. The Collection tab instead displays a card grid across both pages; page arrows or the mouse wheel browse the catalog. Each physical page has a number at the bottom: 1 and 2, then 3 and 4, and so on. Hovering a card lifts it toward the player and turns its face toward the reading camera. **J** or **Escape** closes the book; there is no close button. The last selected entry remains available when reopening.

`JournalBookModel.gd` constructs the leather covers, layered paper, spine, and brass corners. `Journal3D.gd` places text, card illustrations, icons, and clickable controls directly on the 3D pages. Effect icons use Nearest texture filtering. Opening and closing animate both book and camera; switching between chapter tabs turns a physical page while the cover-mounted side tabs remain visible in place. The tabs use a separate node layer and are reused across page changes. Their backgrounds and captions render above the turning sheet so it cannot hide them. Selecting entries, browsing the index or Collection grid, and continuing long entries update immediately. No fullscreen 2D journal interface is used.

Reading temporarily uses a camera-local environment to remove bloom and fog that obscure printed text. The camera's CanvasLayer filter overlays remain active with their original materials and parameters, including blur, pixelation, and grain. Original environment, camera pose, and field of view are restored on normal close, interruption, and scene exit; saved graphics settings are untouched.

Main blocks underlying room, collection, shop, and battle input while the book is active. Reading does not pause the match or network processing. An incoming battle presentation or other camera transition synchronously closes the journal before moving the camera. Pack opening, card inspection, fusion, room travel, and other unfinished actions prevent opening it.

## Content

`JournalContent.build_chapters()` returns independent dictionaries for `cards`, `special_cards`, `event_cards`, `effects`, `mechanics`, `profile`, and `collection`, in that order. The catalog chapters describe authored base data regardless of ownership. The profile reads current progress without changing manager state, creating instances, changing RNG state, or saving anything. The dedicated Event Cards chapter always shows all six `EventDeck.definitions()` entries, sorted by their translated names, with no discovery requirement or NEW badges. Event types are unlocked for everyone from the start and are excluded from packs and permanent collections; matches still grant a random starting event hand and Draw on Kill draws from the event deck.

The Collection tab reserves a fixed slot for every collectible combat and special card once, ordered by rarity from common through secret, then by canonical card ID within each rarity, so language and ownership changes cannot rearrange it. Event cards have no Collection slots, including for saves with older event discovery metadata. Undiscovered cards show only a dashed outline and a question mark. Obtaining a card reveals its normal face in that slot permanently, even after its last copy is consumed. The pages have no chapter heading, index list, description, ownership count, or other explanatory text. Revealed cards keep their normal artwork and printed card details. Hovering a revealed card raises and turns it toward the camera, then returns it to its grid position when the pointer leaves. Clicking it opens its full entry in the combat-card or special-card chapter, including the correct index page. Unknown placeholders remain inactive. Navigation resolves stable entry IDs across rarity sorting and translations. Collection uses independent deep copies of the collectible catalog entries, with IDs such as `collection:card:eg`; browsing or animating them never changes the descriptive chapters or authored card data.

Discovery is stored per save slot in `collection.json` under `metadata.discovered_cards`. Regular acquisitions, exact pack instances, fusion rewards, and Jester rewards record discovery with the acquired card. Event hands, opponent cards and spectator hands do not discover cards; event IDs are excluded from discovery and NEW queries even when older saves contain those IDs. Fusion and Jester transactions publish discovery only after their complete save succeeds. `has_discovered_card()` and `get_discovered_card_ids()` read without migration, instance generation, RNG use, or saving. Older saves seed discovery from currently owned cards and retained Jester offering/reward records; past consumed cards with no remaining historical record cannot be reconstructed. The next ordinary save persists that migration.

During pack opening, a first-ever card type also receives a `NEW` badge after turning face-up. `NewCardLabel.gd` plays `assets/cards/newlabel.png` as 64 × 64 frames at 12 FPS in a loop, with nearest filtering and a camera-facing Sprite3D at the card's upper-left corner. The first revealed copy per pack gets the badge; duplicate copies and previously discovered types do not, including types with no copies left. The badge disappears when collection starts and is freed with its card.

The table collection and Journal Collection show the same animated `NEW` badge on discovered card types until their first hover in either collection or inspection in the table collection's detail view. Opening a detail and scrolling to the previous or next card both acknowledge the displayed type, even without hovering it. Acknowledgement is shared by every copy and both collection views, and survives consumption, reacquisition, and reload. Each save slot stores its own boolean `metadata.seen_cards` map; resetting a slot clears it. Existing discovered cards without recorded viewing history start with the badge. `is_card_new()` reads without modifying state. `mark_card_seen()` saves a snapshot before publishing the acknowledgement through `card_seen`; a failed write leaves the card new. Unknown IDs and already seen cards do not save or emit signals. The separate signal updates badges without rebuilding the grid or replaying acquisition behavior.

The profile tab has an overview, a deck page, and a collection page. Its prominent total strength is the sum of attack and maximum health across every copy in the saved combat deck, including purchased type upgrades. Effects are counted separately rather than assigned an arbitrary combat-power weight. Deck details include average strength, unique card types, effects and effect types, Holo copies, and the strongest card. Collection statistics distinguish copies from unique types and show combat/special completion, Holo copies, and purchased stat upgrades. The overview also identifies the active save slot and shows level, XP, and Soul Coins.

`PlayerProfile.snapshot()` reads the current managers directly. It respects removed innate effects, the two-effect limit, swapped starting stats, and exact saved instance bindings for Holo counts. Old collections are counted without migration or generated instances. Opening the book refreshes the profile; the open profile tab refreshes every half-second while retaining the chosen entry and text page. All profile labels support the five existing languages.

Each chapter contains `id`, `title`, and an `entries` array. Each entry contains `id`, `title`, `subtitle`, and `body`. Card entries also carry the original `card_data`; effect entries carry `icon_path` and `effect_data`. The frontend can render these directly without repeating gameplay descriptions.

Card pages are rebuilt from every `CardDatabase.get_all_cards()` entry, plus the separate `EventDeck.definitions()` list. Existing names and flavor descriptions remain intact. Subtitles identify base stats, German rarity, and special/event categories. Egg, Chick and Chicken include their in-match transformation rules in all five languages. Body pagination measures the actual font height, including fallback accented glyphs, and keeps long translations above the page navigation without dropping text.

The effect chapter includes every database variant, including all three Armor strengths. It additionally includes distinct numeric variants found directly on cards, using `EffectPresentation` for defaults, names, values, rarity, and icon lookup. Effect icons slowly drift, gently rotate and pulse within their dark backing while the book is open; their full bounds remain inside the box. Motion resets when the spread is rebuilt. A missing icon uses the existing effect placeholder. Unknown future effect types retain their authored description.

The mechanics pages cover combat, replacement draws, Egg/Chick/Chicken, collection and saved decks, starting slots, effects/status timing, event rules including Steel, rarity, all pack types, pack quantities, match rewards, level bonuses, Clicker, type upgrades, effect rolls, Holo, fusion/protection, and slots. Room discovery hints remain omitted. Prices, pack contents/levels, reward amounts, deck size, rarity order, and slot stakes/payouts use the existing gameplay data where available.

## Descriptions follow the current game

The authored effect catalog has several older descriptions. Journal text follows `CombatResolver`, `Card3D`, and `GameTable` instead:

- Neighbor Heal triggers at the beginning of the owner's turn.
- Swarm Power counts identical cards on both table sides.
- Stun uses its configured chance and consumes the next attempted attack. Multiple strikes use their configured hit count, clamped to 1–8, with Triple Strike taking priority.
- Cleave uses its configured fraction of attack and is suppressed by Lava. Its damage contributes to Lifesteal, and its kills can trigger Chain Attack.
- Bodyguard takes its configured share of an adjacent ally's raw damage. The living guard on the left takes priority; target and guard use their own armor and shield. The target's shield blocks the whole hit before sharing; redirected damage, poison and thorns are not shared again.
- Draw on Kill draws an event card for an attributed kill, once per dead card. Legacy `deck_burn` data migrates to this effect and is absent from the catalog.
- Poison requires actual hit damage before application. Its ticks and Execute bypass ordinary mitigation but allow Last Stand and Grave Return. Execute is skipped when the same hit already triggered a survival effect, and shielded or zero-damage hits cannot trigger it.
- A destroyed Egg has a 25% chance to hatch a fresh base Chick in the same slot without drawing from the deck or acquiring a collection copy. A failed hatch draws the ordinary replacement; Last Stand and Grave Return can prevent the destruction before any roll.
- Every living Chick, including one drawn normally, matures after five full rounds spent on the table. Each round comprises one full turn by each side; an already-running turn at arrival does not count. Growth pauses while in a draw pile, and Steel preserves the progress already earned.
- Maturation replaces Chick with a fresh base Chicken in the same slot, with no inherited upgrades, Holo, stat changes or status effects. It is not a death, triggers no kill effects, and neither draws from the deck nor adds a collection copy. Both hatched Chicks and matured Chickens receive no Holo roll.
- Steel moves the same living battle instance, preserving its state and Chick growth progress. It is not destruction and cannot hatch an Egg.

Journal and card-effect tooltips share the same parameterized rule descriptions in `EffectPresentation.get_combat_description`. Purchased upgrades remain outside the base-card catalog. The book does not modify any of these gameplay rules.
